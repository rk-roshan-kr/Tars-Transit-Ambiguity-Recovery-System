\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Background}: Automated exoplanet vetting pipelines are typically evaluated using aggregate performance metrics (like AUROC) computed on local, homogeneous test sets. However, these metrics often mask critical failure modes, including probability calibration decay under domain shifts and predictive redundancies in nested models.
\textbf{Objective}: This work operationalizes and evaluates a reproducible, multi-dimensional validation protocol for ambiguity-aware exoplanet vetting systems, demonstrating how comprehensive stress-testing exposes operational boundaries that standard single-metric benchmarks fail to reveal.
\textbf{Methods}: We define a validation protocol encompassing: (1) out-of-domain cross-mission transfer checks, (2) stress-testing under nested regression controls, (3) physical host-star cohort partitioning, (4) resolution-scale noise stress sweeps, and (5) probability calibration diagnostics. We implement and demonstrate this protocol using a baseline event-space vetting pipeline---the Transit Ambiguity Recovery System (TARS), which employs a graph-theoretic Recovery Ambiguity Index (RAI)---evaluated on unblinded cohorts from the TESS, Kepler, and K2 missions ($N = 1780$, $100$, and $41$ targets respectively).
\textbf{Principal Findings}: Applying our protocol reveals several operational limits that aggregate metrics hide. While TARS achieves high discrimination on standard TESS splits, out-of-domain validation on Kepler long-cadence data shows a performance collapse ($\text{AUROC} \approx 0.48$) and severe calibration decay (ECE $= 0.5212$ vs. $0.0966$ on TESS) caused by cadence-induced transit-edge blurring. Nested regression stress-testing (preregistered experiment EXP-019; see Section~3.2, Table~2) exposes that the RAI lacks independent predictive value once controlling for BLS SDE ($p = 0.090$), refuting the metric-only hypothesis but validating the protocol's diagnostic utility. Under physical cohort partitioning, stellar convective noise on giant hosts ($\log g < 4.0$) deforms candidate graph topologies, limiting period recovery.
\textbf{Scope}: These results demonstrate the protocol's diagnostic power on space-mission photometry. Applying the protocol to other independent folding-based and deep-learning detectors is required to fully characterize their operational boundaries.
\textbf{Significance}: Our findings suggest that standard AUROC evaluation underrepresents vetting pipeline vulnerabilities. Operationalizing multi-dimensional validation protocols provides a reproducible, open-science framework for certifying the reliability, calibration, and domain-limits of future exoplanet vetting pipelines before production deployment.
\end{abstract}
